Contracts and Gardens

This page, and every page like it, is produced by an arrangement of four separate decisions that deliberately do not know about each other.

A Document owns content. It says what the source text is, what language that source is in, and nothing about where it appears. A Garden owns membership and languages. It says which documents belong together and which languages that collection is meant to exist in. A Publication intent owns placement. It says that this document, in this garden, in this language, should appear at this path. A View owns presentation, which is the website's concern and not the writer's.

Keeping these apart is what stops a sentence from becoming a layout rule and a theme from becoming content authority. It also means each can change without disturbing the others. Moving a page changes a path in one contract. Adding a language changes a list in another. Neither touches the text.

On top of that sits evidence. A publication in a language other than the document's own is not admissible merely because someone produced a translation. The gate asks two separate questions: does a completed translation exist for this exact revision, and has a person approved that exact revision. A translation of an older revision does not satisfy a newer one, and an approval does not survive the text changing underneath it.

The consequence is that nothing here can drift into being public by accident. Content becomes public because a specific person approved a specific revision, and the record of that approval is what the system checked.
